# oklch-dashboard — Design System

## Overview

A dense, data-first dashboard system built in OKLCH. Everything here is either
an **input** or **derived from one** — that is the organising idea, not a
technical detail.

The YAML above is the single source of truth. Two things generate from it:

| Generated | From | Command |
|---|---|---|
| `app/tokens.css` | `palette` (4 numbers) → `lib/palette/engine.ts` | `pnpm gen:tokens` |
| `colors` / `colors-dark` above | the same engine, mirrored back for readability | `pnpm gen:tokens` |

**Change a colour by changing `palette`, then regenerating.** Never hand-edit
`app/tokens.css`, and never hand-edit the block between the `GENERATED` markers
above. `pnpm gen:tokens --check` exits non-zero when either has drifted, which
makes it a usable pre-commit or CI step.

Everything that is *not* colour — typography, spacing, radius multipliers,
motion, control sizes, density — is authored directly in the YAML above and is
the contract components implement.

---

## Colours

Not a palette. A **grammar**: four inputs (`neutralHue`, `neutralChroma`,
`accentHue`, `contrast`) produce every token in both themes through the same
maths. Light and dark are not two lists that must be kept in sync — they are one
recipe evaluated twice.

What the engine encodes, and why it matters when you read a value:

- **Lightness across a ramp is a quadratic** in ramp position; **chroma is a
  skewed gaussian** that peaks past centre, because saturation does not peak
  politely in the middle.
- **Every hue gets its own curve.** A Helmholtz–Kohlrausch nudge drops lightness
  in proportion to chroma, so saturated hues don't read as lighter than muted
  ones at the same nominal L.
- **Peak chroma is capped per hue.** Blue and purple reach further than amber and
  cyan; a flat ceiling would either clip the blues or starve the yellows.
- **In dark mode chroma is held at zero below L15** and eased in above, so
  mid-dark surfaces don't drift yellow.
- **All four intents share one base lightness.** Verify label contrast once and
  it holds for every intent; the label colour itself is *derived* from the fill's
  lightness rather than chosen.

**Intents mark state, not brand.** `destructive`, `success`, `warning` are for
errors, confirmations and cautions. `primary` is the only brand accent. Each
intent ships in two strengths: the solid fill (`--destructive`) for actions, and
the tonal `-soft` pair (`--destructive-soft` + `--destructive-soft-foreground`)
for badges and passive status — a status badge is information, so it does not
get an action's visual weight.

---

## Typography

One family (Geist), two weights (400 / 500), six roles. There is no display
tier, because **the largest type in this product is a number** — `metric` at
1.25rem is the ceiling, and it is reserved for values.

Emphasis comes from weight and colour, never from a size jump:
`foreground` + 500 is the strongest thing a label can be, `muted-foreground` +
400 the quietest. If something still doesn't stand out, it belongs on a
different surface — not in a bigger font.

All figures carry `tabular-nums`. Numbers that change must not reflow, and
numbers in a column must align — this applies to table cells, KPI values, deltas
and chart tooltips alike. Geist Mono is for identifiers and hashes, not for
emphasis.

---

## Layout

A **shell**, not a page grid: fixed 16rem sidebar, fluid content area, no
centred max-width. The sidebar goes off-canvas below `md`.

The sidebar is a *floating panel* — it sits at `card` elevation, lighter than
the page in both themes, never equal to `--background`.

Spacing works in two layers. The semantic tokens (`screen-edge`,
`between-blocks`, `between-cards`, `between-text`) govern layout-level gaps and
nothing else. A component's internal padding is the component's own concern, and
lives in the `components` and `density` blocks above. Never reach for a semantic
token inside a component.

Wide content — tables, charts, code — scrolls inside its own container. The page
body never scrolls sideways.

---

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is **lightness**, in both themes: closer to the viewer means lighter. The
ladder is `background` → `card` → `popover`, and no surface ever sits at the same
lightness as its parent. `--shadow-soft` is deliberately near-invisible; it
confirms the lightness step, it does not create it.

Lines are not borders. They are stacked box-shadows — fill, then an *inset* ring,
then an inset top highlight, then the drop — so a control can gain a ring on
hover or press without a single pixel of layout shift. See `.btn-raised` in
`app/globals.css`.

---

## Motion

**Instant first.** Two durations: 100ms for state colour changes, 200ms for
enter/exit and the sidebar collapse. 250ms is a hard ceiling — a tool that
animates feels slow, and a dashboard is a tool.

`ease-linear` is the default for width, position and margin; a curve on a layout
transition reads as lag. The standard curve is for colour, opacity and transform.
Animate only `transform` and `opacity` where you have the choice, and always
honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.

The one place motion is physical rather than decorative: a raised button sinks
1px on press while its drop shadow collapses into an inner shadow. That is
feedback, not decoration.

---

## Shapes

One authored radius (`--radius: 0.45rem`) and a multiplier ladder derived from
it in `globals.css`. Softness across the entire product is a single-number edit.
Not responsive — density does not change with viewport here.

Controls are pills (`4xl`, 1.17rem on a 36px control); containers are soft
rectangles (`2xl`). Nesting rule: an inner surface always takes a *smaller* step
than the surface holding it.

---

## Components

Sizes and per-component tokens are in the `control-sizes`, `density` and
`components` blocks above. Two decisions worth stating in words:

**`default` is 36px.** Buttons and inputs share that height so they align in a
toolbar row, and it is one step tighter than a marketing-site control ladder
because screen real estate here belongs to data.

**Only `button-default` is raised.** The solid, sheened, inset-ring treatment
marks the single primary action in a view. Everything else is flat — outline,
secondary, ghost — and `destructive` is tonal rather than solid, because a
destructive action should ask for confirmation, not compete for attention.

Interaction is token-driven, not per-component: `states` for hover/active/
disabled, `focus-ring` on every `:focus-visible`. Never remove a focus outline
without a visible replacement.

---

## Charts

Five series colours, all at the same lightness, so no series outranks another by
accident. **Chart colour is series identity, not status** — a falling line is not
`destructive`. Axis and tooltip figures use the `code` role: mono, tabular,
aligned.

file holds the values; it does not restate the rules.
